## Passage shape

- Length: 300–430 words (typical 360)
- Reading level: US grade 7.5–10.5 (typical 8.5)
- Questions per passage: 8–10 (typical 10), 4 options each
- Figures: 0% of passages, at most 1 per passage

## Styles

### Expository (`expository`, 65%)

An informative article on a general-interest subject. Each paragraph has a clear topic so that questions can be tied to it.

Structure: Numbered paragraphs (usually 3–6) → Paragraph 1 introduces the subject; later paragraphs develop one aspect each → Concrete examples, figures or a short anecdote; brief closing remark

### Argumentative (`argumentative`, 20%)

Makes a case (a social problem and its solutions, a policy proposal). Questions ask about the author's purpose, tone, and the reasons given.

Structure: Problem or claim stated in paragraph 1 → Causes or reasons, with examples → Proposed remedies or policy; closing appeal

### Biographical or historical account (`biographical_narrative`, 10%)

The life of a notable person or the history of an event or monument.

Structure: Introduction of the person or event → Chronological or thematic paragraphs on achievements or developments → Legacy or significance

### Reflective essay (`reflective_essay`, 5%)

A personal, slightly informal essay built on anecdotes.

Structure: Observation about everyday behaviour → One or two anecdotes illustrating it → Moral or reflection

## Difficulty levels

### Easy (90%)

Most questions are answered by locating one sentence in the passage; vocabulary questions use common academic words; distractors are clearly contradicted by the text. Nearly all corpus passages were easy.

Passage markers:
- Plain, concrete prose; short paragraphs; few idioms
- Clear topic sentence in each paragraph

Question markers:
- Retrieve a stated fact or reason
- Choose the closest meaning of a common word in context
- Identify the overall tone from obvious cues

### Medium (10%)

Several questions need inference across paragraphs, the purpose of a detail, or the meaning of a figurative phrase.

Passage markers:
- Abstract ideas or statistics to interpret
- Figurative language or a quoted opinion

Question markers:
- Infer an unstated implication
- Identify the function of an example or analogy

### Hard (0%)

Not used: no hard passages or questions in the corpus.

Passage markers:
- Not used

Question markers:
- Not used

## Question types

| Type | Share | Uses figure | Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detail (`detail`) | 46% | no | Retrieve a fact, reason or example stated in a given paragraph |
| Inference (`inference`) | 25% | no | Infer what the author implies about a person or idea; Interpret a figurative phrase or a quotation |
| Vocabulary in context (`vocabulary_in_context`) | 15% | no | Choose the closest meaning or synonym of a word as used in the passage |
| Main idea and tone (`main_idea_tone`) | 10% | no | Identify the main idea or best title; Identify the author's tone or attitude |
| Negative detail (`negative_detail`) | 2% | no | Identify the item NOT mentioned among a list |
| Purpose of a detail (`rhetorical_purpose`) | 2% | no | Identify why the author includes an example or contrast |

- **Detail:** The most common type; one sentence of the passage answers it.
- **Inference:** Combines statements or reads between the lines.
- **Vocabulary in context:** Asks about one word, quoted from the passage, with four single-word or short-phrase options.
- **Main idea and tone:** Usually the last question(s) of the set.
- **Negative detail:** Stem uses NOT in capitals.
- **Purpose of a detail:** Asks about the function of part of the text.

### Question stems

- Questions follow paragraph order, and stems may name the paragraph ('In paragraph 2, …')
- Vocabulary stems quote the word: 'The word "vital" in paragraph 3 is closest in meaning to'
- Tone and main-idea questions come last

## Distractors

- The opposite of what the passage states
- Plausible real-world facts that the passage does not mention
- A literal reading of a figurative phrase
- Antonyms or unrelated meanings in vocabulary questions
- Extreme wording ('always', 'never', 'only') in wrong options
- A detail from a different paragraph that does not answer the question

## Answer key

- Exactly four options, A–D, one correct
- Options are short phrases or single clauses, parallel in form
- Correct answers spread evenly over A–D (unlike the source book, which favours C)
- Correct-answer positions are balanced across A–D.

## Never do this

- No answer-position bias: never let one letter dominate the key
- No questions that need outside knowledge
- No two near-identical questions in one set
- No figures or tables
- No region-specific detail that a student could not follow without background (the source book often assumes an Indian context); prefer internationally familiar settings
- No 'all of the above' or 'none of the above' options
- No two defensible options; the key must follow from the text
