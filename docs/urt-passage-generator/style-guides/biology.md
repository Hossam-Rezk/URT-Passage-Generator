# URT Biology passage style guide

> Rendered from `server/urt/rules/biology.yaml` (biology-2026.10.0, status **approved**). Edit the YAML, then re-run `python -m corpus_analysis.synthesis check`; do not edit this file.

**Based on:** 13 analyzed passages from 100-passage-based-biology-questions.pdf, ACT Crack Geology.pdf.

**Provenance:** No official URT biology paper was available. 11 passages come from an MCAT-style prep book (100 passage-based biology questions) and 2 from an ACT science book. The prep book differs from the URT in shape (about 9 questions per passage, about 60% of questions need outside knowledge, figures in only about a quarter of passages), so the passage shape here follows the official URT science papers (physics and chemistry): shorter question sets, more data, mostly passage-based questions. Topics, skills and distractor ideas draw on the book. The figure rate is an assumption to review.

## Passage shape

- Length: 220–420 words (typical 300)
- Reading level: US grade 9.5–11.5 (typical 10.5)
- Questions per passage: 5–8 (typical 6), 4 options each
- Figures: 70% of passages, at most 2 per passage

## Styles

### Expository with data (`expository`, 45%)

Explains a biological mechanism and its consequences, supported by data. Questions apply the mechanism to new cases and read the data.

Structure: Introduction of the biological system or condition → Mechanism paragraph (how it works at organ, cell or gene level) → A data table or graph (e.g. activity vs pH, frequency of a trait) → Optional paragraph on a disorder or an application

### Experiment report (`experiment_report`, 27%)

Controlled experiments on organisms, cells or enzymes (e.g. growth on different media after a treatment). Questions ask what each result shows and which experiment tests a hypothesis.

Structure: Background on the organism and conditions → Experiment 1: treatment and results table → Experiment 2: a follow-up that isolates one factor

### Data report (`data_report`, 18%)

A field or survey study summarised in graphs; questions compare years, groups or trends.

Structure: Brief description of the survey or measurement → Two or three figures (time series, distributions)

### Narrative scenario (`narrative_scenario`, 9%)

A case (e.g. a person training in different environments) used to frame physiological questions.

Structure: A person or case introduced in a situation → Sequence of physiological responses described as the situation changes

## Difficulty levels

### Easy (15%)

Answers are stated in the text or read directly from one figure; one step.

Passage markers:
- One mechanism, clearly described
- A single small table or graph

Question markers:
- Identify a stated function or effect
- Read the optimum or the largest value from a figure

### Medium (85%)

Questions combine the mechanism with the data, predict the effect of a change, or work out simple inheritance probabilities. All corpus passages were at this level.

Passage markers:
- A mechanism with several interacting parts (hormones, genes, immune cells)
- Data that must be related to the mechanism

Question markers:
- Predict the physiological response to a change
- Combine two inheritance patterns in a cross
- Judge which observation would weaken a hypothesis

### Hard (0%)

Not used at passage level. About 9% of questions are hard: multi-generation genetics probabilities or chains of physiological cause and effect.

Passage markers:
- Not used at passage level

Question markers:
- Multi-step probability or multi-step causal chain

## Figures

### Table (40%): 3–7 rows × 2–4 columns

- One row per group, genotype, treatment or condition; units in headers

### Line chart (40%): 1–3 series × 5–12 points

- Activity or rate vs pH, temperature or time; each series one enzyme, organism or group

### Bar chart (20%): 1–2 series × 3–8 points

- Counts or frequencies across categories (e.g. size classes, years)

## Question types

| Type | Share | Uses figure | Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inference (`inference`) | 32% | no | Deduce how a regulatory mechanism responds to a change; Predict the consequence of a mutation or disorder |
| Detail (`detail`) | 22% | no | Identify a mechanism or function stated in the text |
| Data interpretation (`data_interpretation`) | 20% | yes | Compare growth or activity across conditions; Relate counts in a figure to the biological process described |
| Concept application (`concept_application`) | 10% | no | Classify a mutation type or a mode of inheritance |
| Hypothesis evaluation (`hypothesis_evaluation`) | 8% | no | Identify evidence that would weaken or support a stated hypothesis |
| Calculation (`calculation`) | 8% | no | Probability of offspring genotypes or phenotypes in a cross |

- **Inference:** Applies the passage's mechanism one step further.
- **Detail:** Answer stated in the text.
- **Data interpretation:** Answered from the table or graph.
- **Concept application:** Basic syllabus concept tied to the passage topic.
- **Hypothesis evaluation:** Tests a claim against evidence.
- **Calculation:** Simple genetics probabilities; no lengthy arithmetic.

### Question stems

- Negative stems are used occasionally and written in capitals ('Which is NOT …')
- Scenario stems restate the situation briefly before asking

## Distractors

- A real mechanism acting in the wrong direction (increase vs decrease, sympathetic vs parasympathetic)
- A neighbouring organ, compartment or molecule (small vs large intestine, the wrong hormone)
- 2×2 grids of paired effects where only one combination is right
- Teleological or Lamarckian explanations of mutation and selection
- Absolute wording ('all', 'always', 'none') in wrong options
- One correct element paired with an irrelevant one

## Answer key

- Exactly four options, A–D, one correct
- Options range from single terms to short sentences, parallel within a question
- Numeric or probability options in ascending order
- Correct-answer positions are balanced across A–D.

## Never do this

- Most questions must be answerable from the passage and its data; at most about a quarter may rely on basic syllabus knowledge (the prep book's many passage-independent recall questions are not URT style)
- No more than 8 questions per passage
- No anatomical drawings, apparatus sketches or pathway diagrams as required figures; describe them in words
- No clinical treatment advice or medical-school-level pathology detail
- No invented technical terms presented as real
- No 'all of the above' or 'none of the above' options
- No two defensible options
