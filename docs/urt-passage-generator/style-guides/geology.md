# URT Geology passage style guide

> Rendered from `server/urt/rules/geology.yaml` (geology-2026.10.0, status **approved**). Edit the YAML, then re-run `python -m corpus_analysis.synthesis check`; do not edit this file.

**Based on:** 26 analyzed passages from ACT Crack Geology.pdf.

**Provenance:** No official URT geology paper was available; all passages come from an ACT science practice book (earth and space science). Its format (data-rich passages, about 5 questions each, three passage types: data representation, research summaries, conflicting viewpoints) matches the official URT science papers closely. ACT labels even-numbered questions F/G/H/J; here all options use A–D. The book also covers astronomy; one astrophysics passage was classified as physics and is not counted.

## Passage shape

- Length: 150–450 words (typical 250)
- Reading level: US grade 8.5–11 (typical 9.5)
- Questions per passage: 4–7 (typical 5), 4 options each
- Figures: 90% of passages, at most 3 per passage

## Styles

### Data representation (`data_report`, 38%)

Mostly figures with little text. Questions read values, identify trends and combine two figures. Usually easy to medium.

Structure: One short paragraph introducing the system and the quantities measured → Two to four figures and tables (profiles, time series, comparisons)

### Research summary (`experiment_report`, 27%)

Controlled studies on soils, rocks, water or models. Questions ask what each study shows, how studies differ, and what result to expect under a new condition (interpolation).

Structure: Background defining terms and the samples (e.g. Soil X and Soil Y) → Experiment or Study 1: method, then a table or graph → Experiment or Study 2 (and 3): another variable, each with its own results

### Conflicting viewpoints (`two_viewpoints`, 19%)

Two scientists (sometimes three) disagree about a cause or a timeline. Questions ask what each would predict, which evidence weakens which view, and where they agree.

Structure: Background on the phenomenon (sometimes with one figure) → Scientist 1: explanation and supporting evidence → Scientist 2: alternative explanation and evidence

### Expository with data (`expository`, 15%)

Explains a set of related processes (weathering types, lake turnover, eclipses and tides) with reference tables or graphs.

Structure: Paragraphs explaining processes in sequence → Reference tables or a graph supporting each part

## Difficulty levels

### Easy (46%)

Direct lookups from one figure or table, or one comparison; at most one simple subtraction.

Passage markers:
- A single study or a set of independent figures
- Monotonic trends; clearly labelled zones or categories

Question markers:
- Read a value or an intersection on a graph
- Identify whether a relationship is direct or inverse

### Medium (54%)

Questions combine two figures or studies, interpolate between conditions, or apply a scientist's criteria to a new case.

Passage markers:
- Several experiments or tables to be related
- Inverted or unusual axes (depth downward, altitude vs temperature)
- Two or three viewpoints with evidence

Question markers:
- Interpolate or extrapolate from the trend
- Combine a table value with a graph value
- Decide how new data affects each viewpoint

### Hard (0%)

Not used at passage level. About 6% of questions are hard: synthesising three sources or reasoning about an assumption behind a study.

Passage markers:
- Not used at passage level

Question markers:
- Identify a hidden assumption, or synthesise three figures

## Figures

### Table (45%): 3–9 rows × 2–5 columns

- Titled 'Table 1', … ; first column is the condition, site, depth or time
- Paired samples are common (Soil X vs Soil Y, Region 1 vs Region 2) as separate columns
- Units in headers (kg/ha, %, m, °C, km)

### Line chart (36%): 1–4 series × 4–15 points

- Profiles may put depth or altitude on the vertical axis, increasing downward or upward as appropriate
- Each series is one site, region, planet or condition, named in a legend
- Time axes may run from past to present

### Bar chart (17%): 1–2 series × 3–8 points

- Bars compare sites, zones or magnitude classes; grouped bars compare two measures

### Scatter plot (2%): 1–3 series × 5–15 points

- Measured values against depth at several sites

## Question types

| Type | Share | Uses figure | Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data interpretation (`data_interpretation`) | 48% | yes | Read values, intersections and boundaries from figures; Identify direct or inverse relationships across tables; Combine information from two figures |
| Inference (`inference`) | 18% | no | Deduce a property from a stated relationship and a definition |
| Detail (`detail`) | 10% | no | Identify a claim made by a given scientist or a stated definition |
| Hypothesis or viewpoint evaluation (`hypothesis_evaluation`) | 10% | no | Assess how new data affects each viewpoint; Decide whether data support a claim |
| Calculation or interpolation (`calculation`) | 8% | yes | Interpolate between adjacent rows or conditions; Compute a difference between two read values |
| Experimental design (`experimental_design`) | 3% | no | Identify why a step was done or how to extend a study |
| Viewpoint comparison (`viewpoint_comparison`) | 3% | no | Identify the main disagreement or a shared assumption |

- **Data interpretation:** The core of geology passages; almost always uses a figure.
- **Inference:** One step beyond the text or data.
- **Detail:** Answer stated in the text.
- **Hypothesis or viewpoint evaluation:** Central to conflicting-viewpoints passages.
- **Calculation or interpolation:** Simple arithmetic or interpolation; no formulas beyond the passage.
- **Experimental design:** About method and controls.
- **Viewpoint comparison:** Asks where two scientists agree or differ.

### Question stems

- Stems name the figure, table, study or scientist ('According to Scientist 2', 'Based on Study 1')
- Prediction stems: 'If Experiment 1 were repeated with …, the result would most likely be …'
- Occasional NOT/EXCEPT stems, written in capitals

## Distractors

- Reverse a direction or correlation (increase vs decrease, deeper vs shallower)
- Use values from an adjacent row, column, zone or grid line
- Extrapolate beyond the data when interpolation is required (or vice versa)
- Swap the variables of two studies or the claims of two scientists
- Absolute wording ('only when', 'independent of') in wrong options
- True statements that do not answer the question asked

## Answer key

- Exactly four options, A–D, one correct
- Numeric options and ranges in ascending order, with units
- Options are short: a term, a value, or a brief directional statement
- Yes/No questions give the reason in each option ('Yes, because …')
- Correct-answer positions are balanced across A–D.

## Never do this

- Questions must be answerable from the passage and figures; outside knowledge is almost never needed (about 2%)
- No maps, cross-sections or apparatus sketches as required figures; describe them in words
- No passages that are only text when the style calls for data
- No data that contradict basic earth science (e.g. temperature rising toward the ocean floor in a normal profile)
- No 'all of the above' or 'none of the above' options
- No two defensible options; every option set must have one answer supported by the data
