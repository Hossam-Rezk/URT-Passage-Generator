**Based on:** 16 analyzed passages from phy _ chem URT 2020.pdf, 100-passage-based-Chemistry-Questions.pdf, ACT Crack Geology.pdf.

**Provenance:** 4 passages come from the official 2020 URT paper; 11 from an MCAT-style prep book (100 passage-based chemistry questions) and 1 from an ACT science book. The prep book is much denser than the URT (about 9 questions per passage, and about 90% of its questions need outside knowledge), so the passage shape here (length, reading level, question count, figure rate, limit on outside knowledge) follows the official URT papers (chemistry and physics), while topics, skills and distractor ideas draw on all sources.

## Passage shape

- Length: 220–420 words (typical 320)
- Reading level: US grade 9.5–12 (typical 10.5)
- Questions per passage: 5–8 (typical 6), 4 options each
- Figures: 90% of passages, at most 3 per passage

## Styles

### Expository with data (`expository`, 46%)

Explains a chemical principle (osmosis, periodic trends, equilibrium) and supports it with data for several named substances. Reactions are written inline as 'Reaction 1', 'Reaction 2'. Questions mix reading the data with applying the stated principle.

Structure: Introduction defining the concept and its terms → Paragraph giving the governing relationship (a formula or a numbered reaction equation) → A table and/or graph of data for the substances discussed → Optional short paragraph on an application

### Experiment report (`experiment_report`, 31%)

Students or researchers run tests on a set of samples (pH, conductivity, precipitation, heating). Results are qualitative (bright/dim, precipitate colour) or numeric. No conclusion paragraph.

Structure: Definitions needed to classify the results → Experiment 1: procedure, then a results table → Experiment 2 (and 3): a different variable or a different test, each with its own table or graph

### Data report (`data_report`, 15%)

Built around figures: rate vs temperature and rate vs pressure, or equilibrium yield across conditions. Questions compare trends and read values.

Structure: Brief context and the quantities measured, with units → Two or more graphs or a two-way table (e.g. yield vs temperature and pressure)

### Historical account of experiments (`historical_account`, 8%)

Two classic experiments in sequence (e.g. on atomic structure). Questions ask which experiment supports which conclusion and what both have in common.

Structure: Experiment 1 by a named scientist: setup, observation, conclusion → Experiment 2 by a later scientist that refines the model

## Difficulty levels

### Easy (19%)

Answers come from a direct lookup in a table or graph, or from a definition in the passage; at most one step.

Passage markers:
- Simple classification tables (e.g. pH ranges)
- One experiment with clear qualitative results

Question markers:
- Match a value to a category in a table
- Identify the trend in one column or one curve

### Medium (81%)

Questions combine the text with a figure, compare curves or tables, or need a short calculation with the passage's formula. Most passages are at this level.

Passage markers:
- A formula or reaction equations plus a data table or graph
- Several substances or conditions to compare
- Two figures with different scales

Question markers:
- Use the given formula or the graph to find a concentration or pressure
- Compare effective concentrations (e.g. including the ionisation factor)
- Predict the effect of a change using the stated principle (e.g. Le Chatelier)

### Hard (0%)

Not used at passage level. About 7% of questions are hard: multi-step reasoning across reactions or applying a principle that is not stated (e.g. a rate vs time inversion).

Passage markers:
- Not used at passage level

Question markers:
- Chain two relationships, or reason from a mechanism to an observable

## Figures

### Table (50%): 3–8 rows × 2–5 columns

- Titled 'Table 1', … ; one row per substance, solution or trial
- Chemical formulas written in standard notation (subscripts as plain digits are acceptable, e.g. MgCl2)
- Units in the header (M, atm, °C, kJ/mol); results may be qualitative words (Bright / Dim / No light)

### Line chart (33%): 1–4 series × 4–10 points

- Each series is one substance or condition, named in a legend
- Physically correct shapes: linear for colligative effects, rising and levelling off for rate vs pressure

### Bar chart (17%): 1–2 series × 3–8 points

- Bars compare a measured quantity across named samples

## Question types

| Type | Share | Uses figure | Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inference (`inference`) | 28% | no | Connect a definition in the text to a trend in the data; Attribute a conclusion to the experiment that supports it |
| Data interpretation (`data_interpretation`) | 26% | yes | Find the largest or smallest value across curves or rows; Classify a sample using a table of ranges |
| Detail (`detail`) | 20% | no | Identify a stated relationship or observation |
| Calculation (`calculation`) | 14% | yes | Use the passage's formula with values from a table; Interpolate or compute a ratio from a graph |
| Concept application (`concept_application`) | 7% | no | Identify oxidation/reduction or the acid/base role in a given equation |
| Hypothesis evaluation (`hypothesis_evaluation`) | 5% | yes | Decide whether new data are consistent with a stated model |

- **Inference:** Applies the passage's principle to a new case.
- **Data interpretation:** Answered from a table or graph.
- **Detail:** Answer stated in the text; usually easy.
- **Calculation:** Short arithmetic; numbers chosen so no calculator is needed.
- **Concept application:** Uses a standard general-chemistry concept tied to the passage; keep to basic syllabus level.
- **Hypothesis evaluation:** Tests a claim against the data, often as 'Yes/No, because …' options.

### Question stems

- Figure questions name the figure or table; reaction questions name the reaction ('In Reaction 2, …')
- Some stems are two-part judgements answered 'Yes, because …' / 'No, because …'

## Distractors

- Reverse the direction of a trend or effect (increase vs decrease, acid vs base)
- Use the value from an adjacent row, category or curve
- Ignore a stated factor (e.g. use nominal molarity instead of effective particle concentration)
- Factor-of-10 slips in unit conversions
- Confuse rate with time, or charge signs of particles
- 2×2 grids of paired properties (strong/weak × acid/base) where only one cell is right
- In 'Yes/No, because' options, pair the right verdict with a wrong reason

## Answer key

- Exactly four options, A–D, one correct
- Numeric options in ascending order, with units
- Formulas and equations in standard notation
- Options short and parallel in structure
- Correct-answer positions are balanced across A–D.

## Never do this

- Most questions must be answerable from the passage and its data; at most about a quarter may rely on basic syllabus knowledge (the prep-book habit of passage-independent questions is not URT style)
- No more than 8 questions per passage
- No figures that cannot be built from data (apparatus sketches, particle diagrams, molecular structures, energy-profile options); describe these in words
- No organic reaction mechanisms or advanced topics beyond general chemistry
- No data that violate chemistry (e.g. pH outside 0–14 for ordinary solutions, inconsistent units)
- No 'all of the above' or 'none of the above' options
- No two defensible options
